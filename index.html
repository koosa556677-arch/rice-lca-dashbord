Emission Hotspot #1: Field Methane

Continuous flooded paddy fields generate anaerobic conditions, causing 43.3%–47.8% of total GHG emissions in the form of methane ($CH_4$). Adopting Alternate Wetting & Drying (AWD) is the main lever for this stage (assumed −35% CH₄; see Methodology & Limitations).

Emission Hotspot #2: Straw Burning

Open field burning of rice straw (69% burning ratio in Central region) contributes ~43% of the regional total (40%–44% by variety), about 4.15M tCO₂e in this model. This depends heavily on the straw-burning emission factor (1.12 kg CO₂e/kg), which appears to include CO₂ and should be verified. Under IPCC guidance biogenic CO₂ is normally excluded (see Methodology & Limitations). Switching to incorporation or bio-char removes burning emissions but incorporated straw can raise field CH₄, so net savings are smaller than the burning figure alone.

Yield Efficiency Impact

High-yielding varieties like Suphan Buri 1 (735 kg/rai) dilute fixed land emissions to achieve 1.60 kg CO₂e/kg, whereas lower-yield varieties like RD6 (356 kg/rai) rise to 3.52 kg CO₂e/kg.

Methodology & Limitations

Scope

  • Functional unit: 1 kg paddy rice. Boundary: cradle-to-farm-gate, Central Region, crop year 2024/25 (2567/68).
  • Included: field CH₄, rice-straw burning, fertilizer, diesel. Excluded: pesticides, seed, irrigation electricity, organic inputs, post-harvest.
  • Method: IPCC 2006/2019 Tier 1 defaults and TGO emission factors; GWP-100 values should be stated explicitly (AR4/AR5/AR6).
  • Status: screening LCA from public data. Not verified or certified under ISO 14067.

Known limitations

  • Straw burning, fertilizer and diesel per rai are identical for every variety (about 502, 131 and 20 kg CO₂e/rai). Differences between varieties come only from CH₄ and yield.
  • Field CH₄ now converts area from rai to hectares (1 rai = 0.16 ha) before applying the kg CH₄/ha/day factor. Earlier versions omitted this and overstated CH₄ about 6.25×.
  • The straw-burning factor (1.12 kg CO₂e/kg) appears to include biogenic CO₂; IPCC Tier 1 CH₄+N₂O only would be roughly 0.09–0.10 kg CO₂e/kg dry matter. This strongly affects the burning share. Verify before reporting.
  • Baseline CH₄ factor 1.30 kg/ha/day is the IPCC 2006 default (2019 Refinement lists a different value); only a water-regime scaling factor (0.85) is applied, with no pre-season or organic-amendment factors.
  • The "Overall" row differs slightly from the sum of varieties (about 0.9% in total emissions).
  • Scenarios are simplified: AWD is an assumed −35% CH₄; zero burning does not add CH₄ from incorporated straw.
  • No uncertainty range is shown. Treat results as relative comparisons and hotspot identification, not precise values.
